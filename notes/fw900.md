This note describes the Sony GDM-FW900 + scaler preset. The preset is an empirical approximation of the spatial and tonal response of the Sony GDM-FW900 computer monitor, fitted to the NIDL report on it. The preset sets `tv.monitor_model = 1`, which selects a separate 1920×1200 deposition path, and 0 keeps the 240-line television model. The Electron beam menu also offers this choice.

Out of scope: a circuit-level reproduction of the FW900 stages.

## Test conditions

- Source data: NIDL report 751810601-120, measured at 1920×1200 and 85 Hz.
- GPU tests: native 1920×1200 test signals, all 9 measurement sites sampled with 1/8-pixel footprints, linear RGBA16F readback.
- Visual check: an unaveraged 1280×800 capture of Contra.
- Machine, display and commit: not recorded.
- Reproduction:

```sh
python3 tools/measurements/build_fw900_model.py --check
cmake --build build -j 8
ctest --test-dir build --output-on-failure -R 'gpu_(fidelity|preset_json|preset|video_chain_helper).*'
build/bin/mynes_gpu --preset presets/sony_gdm_fw900.json path/to/game.nes
```

## Source and signal path

The NIDL report 751810601-120 of 2001-09-06[^nidl] [^ntis] tested the monitor at 1920×1200 and 85 Hz, with an image of 18.870×11.818 inches. The tables and qualifications transcribed from it are in [`fw900_nidl.json`](https://github.com/yaglo/mynes/blob/master/tools/measurements/fw900_nidl.json).

The FW900 cannot take the 15 kHz raster of the NES directly. The preset puts the generic composite decoder of MyNES in front of an explicit external scaler:

1. Decode and filter the NES voltage with the existing receiver.
2. Resample horizontally into 1600 pixels and repeat each NES line 5 times.
3. Place that 1600×1200 image in a 1920×1200 raster with 160-pixel side bars.
4. Apply the measured tone response, then deposit each monitor raster line.
5. Apply the grille, the effective phosphor-primary transform and the host output transfer.

The tube fills a 16:10 viewport, and the game stays 4:3. Horizontal scaling interpolates the voltage linearly, and vertical scaling repeats lines. These scaler choices are assumptions, and no scaler product was measured. The receiver's bandwidth settings in MHz describe the external decoder and do not apply to the high-bandwidth RGB electronics of the FW900. The 240-line gun and spot stages of the TV model are bypassed, so the tube is applied once.

## Values taken from the report

### Tonal response

The tone curve has 256 measured values, interpolated linearly and normalized as `(L(code) - 0.102) / (31.12 - 0.102)` in foot-lamberts. It is a system response measured with changing surrounds, and it does not isolate the gun EOTF. Applying it equally to R, G and B is an assumption, since the report has no per-channel transfer measurements. Input is limited to the measured domain of 0 to 255. The model removes the measured black offset as a whole and does not invent a split between ambient light, leakage and glare.

### Resolution

The report measured the contrast modulation of one-on, one-off grids at 9 sites. Gaussian equivalents are fitted to these grids separately: one for the horizontal video signal, which holds each pixel value for a pixel period, and one for vertical scanline impulses. At the center, sigma is 0.43517 horizontal raster pixels and 0.46480 vertical raster lines. These fits do not form a complete measured MTF, and they do not separate the blur of the video amplifier from that of the beam in a unique way. Support of 4 sigma and analytical integration over the pixel area prevent undersampled scanline flicker.

### Uniformity

The 9 white-field measurements range from 28.2 to 31.1 fL. They are normalized to the center and interpolated bilinearly. The positions use 10% insets, following the report's procedure, and values clamp outside those anchors. No generic vignette is added.

### Grille

The pitch is 0.23 mm at the center and 0.27 mm at the edge, over the image width of 479.298 mm. Quadratic interpolation of the pitch and of its integrated phase keeps the stripes continuous. Stripe widths and gaps are nominal and were not measured on their own. The grille is filtered where the output cannot resolve it, and its physical density is never quantized to a triad of at least 3 host pixels.

### Effective primaries

Table II.22.1 gives R (.602,.344), G (.280,.599) and B (.152,.073), which the preset normalizes to D65 for reproducibility. They are effective coordinates from the test and describe no phosphor spectra. The report's white and color sections disagree with each other, and the preset reproduces its 9200 K test white only approximately. In this mode the white-balance controls use the inverse of the measured tone curve.

## Controls in FW900 mode

The geometry and convergence controls stay available as generic adjustments. The measured model replaces the generic controls for spot width and growth, focus scaling and gamma, and changing them does not refit the measurements. The preset adds no unmeasured dynamic spot growth, regulation, damper-wire dimensions or halation kernel.

The report's halation normalization and patch geometry conflict, as the [Published CRT measurements]({{ '/notes/measurements/' | relative_url }}) note shows, so the preset turns that extra contribution off. The effective transfer and resolution measurements already include effects that these data alone cannot separate.

## Test results

The generator has no dependencies and records every fitted coefficient. The GPU tests feed native 1920×1200 test signals into the same shader, sample all 9 sites with 1/8-pixel footprints and read back linear RGBA16F. The Cm values measured this way differ from the report's by at most 0.0048 (absolute), including integration and half-float rounding, against a tolerance of 0.012. Separate checks cover white uniformity, middle gray, black and the side bars of the external scaler. The energy and spot-growth tests of the legacy TV model still pass.

An unaveraged 1280×800 capture of Contra has none of the broad gaps of a 240-line TV, as expected for this high-resolution PC raster. At ordinary window sizes most of its fine grille averages away. This monitor looks different from a domestic NES TV.

## Limitations

- The preset does not convert to 85 Hz. The source and the host presentation keep their cadence, and applying the report's spatial response at that cadence is an approximation.
- Persistence is off in this preset, because the dataset has no decay measurement.
- The tests show that the implementation matches its fitted targets. They do not validate a complete physical monitor on their own.
