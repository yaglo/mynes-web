[Part 6]({{ '/blog/beam-is-not-a-line/' | relative_url }}) quoted settings of the Basement TV and Late consumer aperture grille presets, and this part goes through one preset, Living Room 1988, parameter by parameter. It models a NES front-loader connected by a composite cable to a 19-inch Zenith shadow mask TV, in a suburban living room with a lamp on.

Note: the numerical tuning and the preset names in this post belong to an earlier prototype. The current Living Room preset is generic and is not calibrated to a Zenith datasheet. Its JSON, reflection settings and evidence limits are documented in the [23-preset audit]({{ '/archive/presets/#living_room_1988' | relative_url }}).

The picture on that TV is a product of specific electronics, and every component in the chain has a datasheet. The cable has a capacitance spec, the TV has a dot pitch from its service manual, and the phosphors have a persistence curve from JEDEC. Each of these changes the image in measurable ways, and each maps to a parameter in the emulator's preset system.

## Parameters of the Living Room 1988 preset

The values below are the Living Room 1988 preset from `frontends/gpu/presets.c`, each with its source.

### Cable

The cable is 2 m of cheap RCA composite cable, like the one bundled in the box. Its capacitance is 80 pF/m, the spec for cheap molded RCA cables with thin foil shielding; decent RG-59 coax has 67 pF/m and quality shielded cables 50 pF/m. Over 2 m the total shunt capacitance is 160 pF. With the source impedance of 75 ohms, it forms a low-pass filter that softens the signal before it reaches the TV.

```c
.video_cable = {
    .length_meters       = 2.0f,
    .capacitance_per_m   = 80e-12f,    /* 80 pF/m (cheap RCA) */
    .connector_resistance = 0.3f,       /* nickel-plated RCA */
    .impedance           = 75.0f,
    .shield_effectiveness = 0.85f,
},
```

The connector resistance is 0.3 ohms, the value for nickel-plated RCA connectors: average consumer connectors in decent condition. Gold-plated connectors have 0.05 ohms, and corroded ones 3.0 ohms or more. A shield effectiveness of 0.85 lets 15% of external interference through, which shows as mild snow that does not distract from play.

### TV input

The TV has no comb filter. A 19-inch Zenith was a budget set in 1988, and it separates luma from chroma with simple bandpass filters. The chroma bandwidth is 0.4 MHz, narrow enough for colors to bleed, and the luma bandwidth is an adequate 4.2 MHz. For comparison, a Sony Wega with its 2-line comb filter gets 1.0 MHz of chroma bandwidth, and a PVM on S-Video gets 1.3 MHz.

```c
.chroma_bandwidth   = 0.4e6f,
.luma_bandwidth     = 4.2e6f,
```

### Hue offset

The hue offset is 2.0 degrees. My hypothesis is that most consumer sets kept the tint setting they left the factory with. At 2 degrees the shift is barely perceptible and makes reds a little warmer.

### Gamma

The gamma is 2.45. Standard CRT gamma for P22 phosphors is 2.40, and this consumer set has some age, so its gamma has drifted up by 0.05. A PVM calibrated to spec sits at 2.20, and the Basement TV preset goes up to 2.50.

### Shadow mask

The shadow mask has a 0.60 mm dot pitch, from the Zenith service manual for a 19-inch shadow mask tube. A smaller pitch is sharper. A PVM's aperture grille has a 0.31 mm pitch, a 25-inch arcade monitor has a 0.28 mm shadow mask, and a cheap 13-inch portable has 0.80 mm. The mask pitch controls how visible the phosphor structure is at viewing distance.

### Color temperature

The white point is 6500 K (D65), the NTSC standard. Japanese TVs of the time ran at 9300 K (D93), which is visibly bluer, so the Famicom Kitchen preset sets `color_temperature = 9300.0f`.

### Coupling capacitor

The coupling capacitor is 10 microfarads, from the NES-001 motherboard schematic. This DC-blocking capacitor sits between the output of the 2C02 PPU and the composite video jack. With a load impedance of 10 kilohms, the cutoff frequency is about 1.6 Hz: low enough to pass all video content and high enough to block DC offset. The Famicom uses a 100 microfarad capacitor with a cutoff at 0.16 Hz, which changes the low-frequency response a little.

```c
.console_coupling_R = 75.0f,
.console_coupling_C = 10e-6f,       /* NES front-loader */
```

### Ambient light

Ambient light is 0.08, for the lamp that is on in the living room. It lifts the black level a little and reduces the perceived contrast. The Retro Gaming Setup preset uses an ambient level of 0 for a dark room, and the Famicom Kitchen uses 0.12 for a fluorescent overhead light.

### Noise

Noise is 0.035, which gives mild snow: visible when you look for it, and invisible once you are playing. The `shield_effectiveness` of 0.85 lets enough interference through the cable to produce this level. The Basement TV preset raises noise to 0.080.

### Hum bar

The hum bar amplitude is 0.02, because the VCR is on the same power strip. Mains hum at 60 Hz couples into the video signal as a slowly rolling brightness band. At 0.02 it shows only on solid-color screens. The Basement TV has a ground loop through an extension cord and uses 0.08.

### Convergence

Static convergence error is 0.15 and dynamic convergence error 0.08. The 3 electron beams (red, green and blue) do not land on exactly the same spot. Static misconvergence is a fixed offset, and dynamic misconvergence varies across the screen and is worse at the edges. These values give mild color fringes on high-contrast edges, with red shifted right by 2.5 signal samples and blue shifted left by 2.0. The fringes show on white text on a black background when the game is paused.

```c
.convergence_static   = 0.15f,
.convergence_dynamic  = 0.08f,
.conv_r_x = 2.5f, .conv_r_y = 0.8f,
.conv_b_x = -2.0f, .conv_b_y = -0.6f,
```

### Halation

Halation is 0.06. Bright areas of the phosphor screen scatter light through the glass faceplate and put a soft halo around bright sprites. At 0.06 the halo is faint: it shows around Mario's white gloves against a dark background and is invisible in most gameplay.

### Barrel distortion

Barrel distortion is 0.04 for the curved tube face, and lines near the edges bow outward. At 0.04 the curvature is visible and moderate. The Bedroom RF preset uses 0.05 for its smaller, more curved tube.

## Studio PVM preset

The Studio PVM preset models the monitor that reviewers and YouTube retro channels show. Its input is S-Video, where luma and chroma arrive separated and need no comb filter, and its aperture grille has a 0.31 mm pitch. The white point is a calibrated D65, `beam_sharpness` is at its maximum of 1.00, noise and hum are zero, and the gold-plated connectors have 0.05 ohms.

```c
.connection      = VIDEO_CONN_SVIDEO,
.comb_type       = VIDEO_COMB_BYPASS,
.video_cable = {
    .connector_resistance = 0.05f,   /* gold-plated mini-DIN */
    .shield_effectiveness = 0.95f,
},
.tv = {
    .chroma_bandwidth   = 1.3e6f,
    .beam_sharpness     = 1.00f,
    .noise_level        = 0.0f,
    .hum_bar_amplitude  = 0.0f,
    .gamma              = 2.20f,    /* calibrated */
},
```

## Basement TV preset

The Basement TV preset has 3 m of corroded coax. The connector resistance is 3.0 ohms, for F-connectors with green patina. A shield effectiveness of 0.45 lets more than half of external interference through. A ghost delay of 32 samples at a ghost level of 0.12 gives a visible double image, from signal reflections in the corroded connector. The RF noise floor of -44 dBm gives heavy snow.

```c
.video_cable = {
    .connector_resistance = 3.0f,
    .shield_effectiveness = 0.45f,
    .ghost_delay          = 32,
    .ghost_level          = 0.12f,
},
.tv = {
    .beam_sharpness       = 0.00f,   /* completely defocused */
    .convergence_static   = 0.40f,
    .conv_r_x = 6.0f, .conv_b_x = -5.0f,
    .noise_level          = 0.080f,
    .hum_bar_amplitude    = 0.08f,
},
.rf = {
    .noise_floor_dbm  = -44.0f,
},
```

The convergence error is large: red is shifted 6.0 samples right and blue 5.0 samples left. With `beam_sharpness` at 0.0, the beam is completely defocused. Every setting is far from spec, so every stage of the pipeline is stressed. This is the preset that tests edge cases.

## Presets as physical configurations

The NES picture that people saw depended on the electronics between the console and their eyes. The same game on 2 NES consoles connected to different TVs in different rooms looks different in ways that can be measured and turned into parameters. Each of the 9 presets in `presets.c` models one physical configuration of console, cable, TV and room.

The parameters are physical values in pF/m, MHz, ohms, millimeters and kelvins. They are harder to tune by hand than perceptual sliders labeled "blur" and "color bleed", and easier to verify against datasheets.
